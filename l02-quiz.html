#Mock Quiz 2A

Question 1 (4 marks)

Decide whether each statement is true or false. If it is false, give a counter-example or a counter-statement.

  1. A tall matrix A∈Rm×nA\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}A∈Rm×n with m>nm>nm>n can have a right inverse.
  2. If AAA is square and XA=IXA=IXA=I, then AX=IAX=IAX=I.
  3. tr⁡(AB)=tr⁡(BA)\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA)tr(AB)=tr(BA) for all A∈Rm×nA\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}A∈Rm×n and B∈Rn×mB\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}B∈Rn×m.
  4. If AAA and BBB are symmetric, then ABABAB is symmetric.
Solution & marking
  1. False. A right inverse requires independent rows, but m>nm>nm>n rows in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn are always dependent. For example, with A=(10)A=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}A=(10​), every product AXAXAX has second row zero, so AX≠I2AX\neq I_2AX=I2​.
  2. True. A square matrix with a left inverse has independent columns and is therefore invertible; and since X=X(AY)=(XA)Y=YX=X(AY)=(XA)Y=YX=X(AY)=(XA)Y=Y, the left inverse is the inverse.
  3. True.
    tr⁡(AB)=∑i=1m∑k=1nAikBki=∑k=1n∑i=1mBkiAik=tr⁡(BA).\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\sum_{i=1}^m\sum_{k=1}^n A_{ik}B_{ki}=\sum_{k=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^m B_{ki}A_{ik}=\operatorname{tr}(BA).tr(AB)=i=1∑m​k=1∑n​Aik​Bki​=k=1∑n​i=1∑m​Bki​Aik​=tr(BA).
  4. False. With A=(1000)A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}A=(10​00​) and B=(0110)B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}B=(01​10​),
    AB=(0100),AB=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},AB=(00​10​),
    which is not symmetric. The product of symmetric matrices is symmetric only when they commute.

(1 mark each: half for the verdict, half for the justification.)

Question 3 (4 marks)
  1. Prove that if AAA has linearly independent columns, then A⊤AA^\top AA⊤A is invertible. (2 marks)
  2. Prove that if QQQ has orthonormal columns, then ∥Qx−Qy∥=∥x−y∥\|Qx-Qy\|=\|x-y\|∥Qx−Qy∥=∥x−y∥ for all x,yx,yx,y. (2 marks)
Solution & marking
  1. Suppose A⊤Ax=0A^\top Ax=\mathbf 0A⊤Ax=0. Multiplying on the left by x⊤x^\topx⊤,
    0=x⊤A⊤Ax=(Ax)⊤(Ax)=∥Ax∥2,0=x^\top A^\top Ax=(Ax)^\top(Ax)=\|Ax\|^2 ,0=x⊤A⊤Ax=(Ax)⊤(Ax)=∥Ax∥2,
    so Ax=0Ax=\mathbf 0Ax=0, and independence of the columns gives x=0x=\mathbf 0x=0. A square matrix whose null space is {0}\{\mathbf 0\}{0} is invertible.
  2. Orthonormal columns mean Q⊤Q=IQ^\top Q=IQ⊤Q=I. Therefore
    ∥Qx−Qy∥2=∥Q(x−y)∥2=(x−y)⊤Q⊤Q (x−y)=(x−y)⊤(x−y)=∥x−y∥2,\|Qx-Qy\|^2=\|Q(x-y)\|^2=(x-y)^\top Q^\top Q\,(x-y)=(x-y)^\top(x-y)=\|x-y\|^2 ,∥Qx−Qy∥2=∥Q(x−y)∥2=(x−y)⊤Q⊤Q(x−y)=(x−y)⊤(x−y)=∥x−y∥2,
