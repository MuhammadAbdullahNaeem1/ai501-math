#Mock Quiz 6A

Question 1 (5 marks)

Let A=[5222]A=\begin{bmatrix}5&2\\2&2\end{bmatrix}A=[52​22​].

  1. Find the eigenvalues, and check them against the trace and the determinant. (2 marks)
  2. Find orthonormal eigenvectors and write A=QΛQ⊤A=Q\Lambda Q^\topA=QΛQ⊤. (2 marks)

  1. With tr⁡A=7\operatorname{tr}A=7trA=7 and det⁡A=10−4=6\det A=10-4=6detA=10−4=6,
    p(λ)=λ2−7λ+6=(λ−6)(λ−1),soλ1=6,λ2=1.p(\lambda)=\lambda^2-7\lambda+6=(\lambda-6)(\lambda-1),\qquad\text{so}\qquad \lambda_1=6,\quad \lambda_2=1 .p(λ)=λ2−7λ+6=(λ−6)(λ−1),soλ1​=6,λ2​=1.
    As checks, 6+1=7=tr⁡A6+1=7=\operatorname{tr}A6+1=7=trA and 6⋅1=6=det⁡A6\cdot1=6=\det A6⋅1=6=detA.
  2. For λ=6\lambda=6λ=6,
    (A−6I)v=[−122−4]v=0 ⟹ v1=2v2,q1=15[21];(A-6I)v=\begin{bmatrix}-1&2\\2&-4\end{bmatrix}v=\mathbf 0\ \Longrightarrow\ v_1=2v_2,\qquad q_1=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix};(A−6I)v=[−12​2−4​]v=0 ⟹ v1​=2v2​,q1​=5​1​[21​];
    for λ=1\lambda=1λ=1,
    (A−I)v=[4221]v=0 ⟹ v2=−2v1,q2=15[1−2].(A-I)v=\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}v=\mathbf 0\ \Longrightarrow\ v_2=-2v_1,\qquad q_2=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{bmatrix}1\\-2\end{bmatrix}.(A−I)v=[42​21​]v=0 ⟹ v2​=−2v1​,q2​=5​1​[1−2​].
    Therefore
    A=15[211−2][6001]15[211−2].A=\frac{1}{\sqrt5}\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&-2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}6&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt5}\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&-2\end{bmatrix}.A=5​1​[21​1−2​][60​01​]5​1​[21​1−2​].
  3. AAA is symmetric, so by the spectral theorem its eigenvectors are orthonormal, which means Q⊤Q=IQ^\top Q=IQ⊤Q=I.

#Mock Quiz 6C

Question 1 (4 marks)

A symmetric 2×22\times22×2 matrix has eigenvalue 444 with eigenvector (1,2)(1,2)(1,2), and eigenvalue −1-1−1.

  1. Find the other eigenvector without computation, and explain why. (1 mark)
  2. Reconstruct the matrix. (3 marks)
Solution & marking
  1. (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), or any non-zero multiple of it: for a symmetric matrix, eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.
  2. Normalise the eigenvectors and assemble A=QΛQ⊤A=Q\Lambda Q^\topA=QΛQ⊤ as a sum of rank-one pieces:
    A=4⋅15[12][12]+(−1)⋅15[2−1][2−1]=15([48816]−[4−2−21])=15[0101015]=[0223].A=4\cdot\frac15\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&2\end{bmatrix}+(-1)\cdot\frac15\begin{bmatrix}2\\-1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\end{bmatrix} =\frac15\left(\begin{bmatrix}4&8\\8&16\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}4&-2\\-2&1\end{bmatrix}\right)=\frac15\begin{bmatrix}0&10\\10&15\end{bmatrix}=\boxed{\begin{bmatrix}0&2\\2&3\end{bmatrix}}.A=4⋅51​[12​][1​2​]+(−1)⋅51​[2−1​][2​−1​]=51​([48​816​]−[4−2​−21​])=51​[010​1015​]=[02​23​]​.
    Checks. A(1,2)⊤=(4,8)⊤=4 (1,2)⊤A(1,2)^\top=(4,8)^\top=4\,(1,2)^\topA(1,2)⊤=(4,8)⊤=4(1,2)⊤; tr⁡A=3=4+(−1)\operatorname{tr}A=3=4+(-1)trA=3=4+(−1); and det⁡A=−4=4⋅(−1)\det A=-4=4\cdot(-1)detA=−4=4⋅(−1).
Question 2 (3 marks)

Describe geometrically, using its eigen-decomposition, what S=[2112]S=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}S=[21​12​] does to the unit circle. What is the area of the image?

Solution & marking

S=Qdiag⁡(3,1)Q⊤S=Q\operatorname{diag}(3,1)Q^\topS=Qdiag(3,1)Q⊤, where QQQ is a rotation by 45∘45^\circ45∘. The map rotates by −45∘-45^\circ−45∘ (bringing the diagonal onto the xxx-axis), stretches horizontally by 333, and rotates back. The unit circle therefore becomes an ellipse with semi-axis 333 along (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) and semi-axis 111 along (−1,1)(-1,1)(−1,1), and its area is
π⋅3⋅1=3π=∣det⁡S∣ π.\pi\cdot3\cdot1=3\pi=|\det S|\,\pi .π⋅3⋅1=3π=∣detS∣π.
