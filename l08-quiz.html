Question 2 (2 marks)

A point is drawn uniformly from the unit ball in Rd\mathbb R^dRd. Find the probability that it lies within ϵ\epsilonϵ of the surface for (a) d=20d=20d=20, ϵ=0.1\epsilon=0.1ϵ=0.1; (b) d=100d=100d=100, ϵ=0.02\epsilon=0.02ϵ=0.02. What does this say about nearest-neighbour methods?

Solution & marking

The shell fraction is 1−(1−ϵ)d1-(1-\epsilon)^d1−(1−ϵ)d: (a) 1−0.920≈1−0.122=0.8781-0.9^{20}\approx1-0.122=0.8781−0.920≈1−0.122=0.878; (b) 1−0.98100≈1−0.133=0.8671-0.98^{100}\approx1-0.133=0.8671−0.98100≈1−0.133=0.867. Most of the volume sits in a thin shell, so in high dimensions almost all points are at nearly the same distance from any query point, and "nearest" stops meaning much.

Question 3 (2 marks)

  1. The PCA scores along different principal components are uncorrelated.
  2. Dividing the covariance by n−1n-1n−1 instead of nnn changes the principal components.

Question 2 (3 marks)

Two features are measured in very different units (income in dollars, age in years).

  1. What goes wrong if PCA is applied to the raw features? (1 mark)
  2. What should be done instead, and what matrix is then decomposed? (1 mark)
  3. After this step the two features have correlation ρ=0.6\rho=0.6ρ=0.6. Find the eigenvalues and the fraction kept by one component. (1 mark)
Solution & marking

  2. Standardise each feature (subtract its training-set mean, divide by its standard deviation); PCA then decomposes the correlation matrix R=[1ρρ1]R=\begin{bmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{bmatrix}R=[1ρ​ρ1​].
