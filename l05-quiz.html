Question 3 (2 marks)

Decide whether each statement is true or false, with a reason.

  1. A polynomial of degree 999 fitted to 101010 points with distinct xxx-values has zero training error.
  2. For the same value of λ\lambdaλ, the lasso always produces a sparser solution than ridge.
Solution & marking
  1. True. There are ten parameters and ten points, and the square Vandermonde matrix is invertible, so the polynomial interpolates the data exactly — a textbook case of overfitting.
  2. False as stated. The two λ\lambdaλ's are not on the same scale, and the lasso sets nothing to zero if every ∣bj∣>λ|b_j|>\lambda∣bj​∣>λ. What is true is that the lasso can produce exact zeros, whereas ridge never does for finite λ\lambdaλ.

Question 2 (3 marks)

A data set has 100100100 points, and the true relationship is linear. You fit both a linear and a cubic model.

  1. Which model has the lower training RSS? (1 mark)
  2. Which do you expect to have the lower test RSS? (1 mark)
  3. If the truth were mildly non-linear, could you say in advance which model has the lower test RSS? (1 mark)
Solution & marking
  1. The cubic (or a tie): the linear model is the special case of the cubic with the extra coefficients zero, so the cubic can never fit the training data worse.
  2. The linear model: the cubic's extra terms add variance without reducing bias.
  3. No. The answer depends on the balance between bias and variance, which must be settled with validation data.
Question 3 (3 marks)

Describe the constraint regions of ridge and lasso for two coefficients, and explain why the lasso gives exact zeros while ridge does not. What is the elastic net?

Solution & marking
  • Ridge constrains the coefficients to the disc θ12+θ22≤t\theta_1^2+\theta_2^2\le tθ12​+θ22​≤t. The disc is smooth, so the error ellipses touch it at a generic point, where both coefficients are non-zero.
  • The lasso constrains them to the diamond ∣θ1∣+∣θ2∣≤t|\theta_1|+|\theta_2|\le t∣θ1​∣+∣θ2​∣≤t, whose corners lie on the axes. The ellipses usually make first contact at a corner, where one coefficient is exactly zero — a sparse solution.
  • The elastic net minimises
    12∥y−Xθ∥2+λ1∥θ∥1+λ22∥θ∥22,\tfrac12\|y-X\theta\|^2+\lambda_1\|\theta\|_1+\tfrac{\lambda_2}{2}\|\theta\|_2^2 ,21​∥y−Xθ∥2+λ1​∥θ∥1​+2λ2​​∥θ∥22​,
    which contains ridge and the lasso as special cases and has two hyper-parameters, both tuned on validation data.
Remember

The regression quiz recipe: build XXX with a column of ones; form X⊤XX^\top XX⊤X and X⊤yX^\top yX⊤y; invert the 2×22\times22×2 matrix; compute www; and check that the residuals sum to zero (and that X⊤e=0X^\top e=\mathbf 0X⊤e=0). For ridge, add λ\lambdaλ to the diagonal first.
