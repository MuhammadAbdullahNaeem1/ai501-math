Question 3 (2 marks)

True/False with a reason: (a) A degree-9 polynomial fitted to 10 points with distinct xxx has zero training error. (b) Lasso always produces a sparser solution than ridge for the same λ\lambdaλ.

Solution & marking

(a) True — 10 parameters, 10 points, square invertible Vandermonde matrix ⇒ exact interpolation (overfitting).
(b) False as stated — the two λ\lambdaλ's aren't on the same scale and lasso may set nothing to zero if all ∣bj∣>λ|b_j|>\lambda∣bj​∣>λ; but lasso can produce exact zeros while ridge (for finite λ\lambdaλ) never does.

Question 2 (3 marks)

A dataset of 100 points; true relationship is linear. You fit a linear and a cubic model.
(a) Which has lower training RSS? (1) (b) Which do you expect to have lower test RSS? (1) (c) If the truth were mildly non-linear, can you tell which has lower test RSS? (1)

Solution & marking

(a) Cubic (≤), since linear is a special case of cubic (nested). (b) Linear — the cubic adds variance without reducing bias. (c) Not determinable in advance; bias–variance balance — use validation.

Question 3 (3 marks)

Sketch (in words) the constraint regions for ridge and lasso with two coefficients and explain why lasso gives exact zeros but ridge does not. What is the elastic net?

Solution & marking

Ridge: disc θ12+θ22≤t\theta_1^2+\theta_2^2\le tθ12​+θ22​≤t — smooth, error ellipses touch it at a generic point (both weights non-zero). Lasso: diamond ∣θ1∣+∣θ2∣≤t|\theta_1|+|\theta_2|\le t∣θ1​∣+∣θ2​∣≤t with corners on the axes; the ellipses usually first touch a corner, where one coefficient is exactly 0 ⇒ sparse. Elastic net: 12∥y−Xθ∥2+λ1∥θ∥1+λ22∥θ∥2\tfrac12\|y-X\theta\|^2+\lambda_1\|\theta\|_1+\tfrac{\lambda_2}2\|\theta\|^221​∥y−Xθ∥2+λ1​∥θ∥1​+2λ2​​∥θ∥2 — both penalties; ridge and lasso are special cases; two hyper-parameters tuned on validation data.
