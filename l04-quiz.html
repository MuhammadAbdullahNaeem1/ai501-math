#Mock Quiz 4A

Question 1 (2 marks)

Classify each task as regression, classification or neither (half a mark each).

  1. Predicting a house's sale price from its features.
  2. Predicting whether an email is spam.
  3. Predicting the probability that a patient is readmitted within 30 days.
  4. Discovering groups of similar customers when no labels are available.
Solution & marking
  1. Regression.
  2. Classification.
  3. Regression — the output is a real number in [0,1][0,1][0,1].
  4. Neither — this is unsupervised clustering.

#Mock Quiz 4B

Question 1 (3 marks)

The labels of five training samples, to be fitted by a constant predictor h(x)=ch(x)=ch(x)=c, are {1,2,2,3,12}\{1,2,2,3,12\}{1,2,2,3,12}.

  1. Find the value of ccc that minimises the MSE and the value that minimises the MAE. (2 marks)
  2. Which loss would you use if the label 121212 is probably a recording error? Why? (1 mark)
Solution & marking
  1. The MSE is minimised by the mean, c=1+2+2+3+125=4c=\tfrac{1+2+2+3+12}{5}=\boxed{4}c=51+2+2+3+12​=4​, and the MAE by the median, c=2\boxed{c=2}c=2​.
  2. The MAE. It grows only linearly with the error, so a single wild label barely moves its minimiser (the median), whereas under the squared loss the outlier drags the mean towards it.
Question 2 (4 marks)

Two features are age (years) and monthly income (thousands of rupees). The training points are P1=(30,50)P_1=(30,50)P1​=(30,50) in class A and P2=(32,200)P_2=(32,200)P2​=(32,200) in class B, and the test point is T=(60,60)T=(60,60)T=(60,60).

  2. Apply min–max scaling using the ranges of the three points (age [30,60][30,60][30,60], income [50,200][50,200][50,200]), then predict again. (2 marks)

  1. On the raw features,
    d2(T,P1)=302+102=1000,d2(T,P2)=282+1402=784+19 600=20 384,d^2(T,P_1)=30^2+10^2=1000,\qquad d^2(T,P_2)=28^2+140^2=784+19\,600=20\,384,d2(T,P1​)=302+102=1000,d2(T,P2​)=282+1402=784+19600=20384,
    so the nearest neighbour is P1P_1P1​ and the prediction is A.
  2. After scaling, P1=(0,0)P_1=(0,0)P1​=(0,0), P2=(230,1)≈(0.067,1)P_2=\big(\tfrac{2}{30},1\big)\approx(0.067,1)P2​=(302​,1)≈(0.067,1) and T=(1,10150)≈(1,0.067)T=\big(1,\tfrac{10}{150}\big)\approx(1,0.067)T=(1,15010​)≈(1,0.067). Then

  3. The scaling statistics (minimum and maximum, or mean and standard deviation) must be computed on the training set only and then applied unchanged to the test points; using test data would leak information. (The question uses all three points only to keep the arithmetic simple.)
Question 3 (3 marks)

Decide whether each statement is true or false, with a one-line reason.

  1. Increasing kkk in kNN increases the variance.
  2. The training error of 1-NN on its own training set is 000 (assuming no duplicate points).
  3. The prediction cost of kNN is O(dn)O(dn)O(dn) per query, and its training cost is negligible.
Solution & marking
  1. False. A larger kkk averages over more neighbours, giving a smoother boundary: lower variance and higher bias.
  2. True. Every training point is its own nearest neighbour.
  3. True. Each query computes distances to all nnn points in ddd dimensions, while training merely stores the data.

Question 3 (2 marks)

Suppose there are ppp independent features, each uniform on [0,1][0,1][0,1]. What side length LLL must a cube-shaped neighbourhood have to contain 1%1\%1% of the data when p=1p=1p=1 and when p=50p=50p=50? What does this say about kNN?

Solution & marking

We need Lp=0.01L^p=0.01Lp=0.01, so L=0.011/pL=0.01^{1/p}L=0.011/p:
p=1: L=0.01;p=50: L=0.011/50=e(ln⁡0.01)/50≈e−0.092≈0.912.p=1:\ L=0.01;\qquad p=50:\ L=0.01^{1/50}=e^{(\ln 0.01)/50}\approx e^{-0.092}\approx0.912 .p=1: L=0.01;p=50: L=0.011/50=e(ln0.01)/50≈e−0.092≈0.912.
In fifty dimensions the "neighbourhood" must span 91%91\%91% of the range of every feature, so the neighbours are no longer close to the query and kNN's assumption that nearby points have similar labels breaks down — the curse of dimensionality.

Remember

For a kNN question, list every distance, sort them, state the tie rule and vote — and remember that scaling can change the answer. For a confusion-matrix question, find PPP and NNN from the prevalence first, then fill in TP and FN from the recall, and FP and TN from the specificity or the precision.
