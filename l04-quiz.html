#Mock Quiz 4A

Question 1 (2 marks)

Tick Regression / Classification / Neither (0.5 each).
(i) Predicting a house's sale price from its features.
(ii) Predicting whether an email is spam.
(iii) Predicting the probability that a patient is readmitted within 30 days.
(iv) Discovering groups of similar customers with no labels.

Solution & marking

(i) Regression. (ii) Classification. (iii) Regression (a real number in [0,1][0,1][0,1]). (iv) Neither (unsupervised clustering).

#Mock Quiz 4B

Question 1 (3 marks)

Labels of five training samples for a constant predictor h(x)=ch(x)=ch(x)=c: {1,2,2,3,12}\{1,2,2,3,12\}{1,2,2,3,12}.
(a) Find the ccc minimising MSE and the ccc minimising MAE. (2)

Solution & marking

(a) MSE ⇒ mean =20/5=4=20/5=4=20/5=4. MAE ⇒ median =2=2=2.
(b) MAE: it grows linearly so a single wild label barely moves the minimiser (median), while squared loss lets the outlier drag the mean.

Question 3 (3 marks)

State True/False with one line each.
(a) Increasing kkk in kNN increases variance.
(b) Training error of 1-NN on its own training set is 0 (no duplicate points).
(c) The prediction cost of kNN per query is O(dn)O(dn)O(dn) and the training cost is negligible.

Solution & marking

(a) False — larger kkk averages more neighbours ⇒ smoother boundary ⇒ lower variance, higher bias.
(b) True — each training point is its own nearest neighbour.
(c) True — distances to all nnn points in ddd dimensions; training only stores the data.

Question 3 (2 marks)

With ppp independent features uniform on [0,1][0,1][0,1], what side length LLL must a cube neighbourhood have to contain 1% of the data for p=1p=1p=1 and p=50p=50p=50? What does this say about kNN?

Solution & marking

Lp=0.01⇒L=0.011/pL^p=0.01\Rightarrow L=0.01^{1/p}Lp=0.01⇒L=0.011/p: p=1p=1p=1: L=0.01L=0.01L=0.01; p=50p=50p=50: L=0.010.02=e−0.092≈0.912L=0.01^{0.02}=e^{-0.092}\approx0.912L=0.010.02=e−0.092≈0.912. In 50 dimensions the "neighbourhood" spans 91% of every feature's range — neighbours are no longer close, so kNN's similarity assumption fails (curse of dimensionality).

Remember

kNN quiz recipe: list every distance, sort, state the tie rule, vote; scaling can flip the answer. Confusion-matrix recipe: get PPP and NNN from prevalence first, then fill TP/FN from recall and FP/TN from specificity or precision.
